Master's thesis · M.Sc. Data Science · 2026

Evaluating Adaptive Timestep Sampling for the Efficient Training of Flow Matching Models

Computer Vision & Learning group (CompVis), Faculty of Mathematics, Computer Science and Statistics, Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München · Supervisor: Prof. Dr. Björn Ommer · Advisor: Felix Krause, M.Sc. · Submitted 6 August 2026

Download thesis · PDF Defence slides · PDF Back to research

Abstract

Flow Matching has emerged as a simulation-free successor to diffusion models that preserves sample quality while enabling fewer-step inference on straight Conditional Optimal-Transport (CondOT) paths. As such models scale, training has become increasingly compute-intensive, making the allocation of training iterations across the timestep axis of the conditional flow matching objective a central concern. Uniform timestep sampling is known to be suboptimal, which motivated static non-uniform densities such as the logit-normal prior. These, however, cannot react to the evolving capacity of the network as it learns. The reinforcement-learning-based Adaptive Timestep Sampling (ATS) addresses exactly this deficit for denoising diffusion.

This thesis asks whether ATS transfers from discrete ε-prediction diffusion to continuous CondOT velocity regression. We port ATS to Flow Matching, propose a variance-reduced Advantage Actor-Critic variant (A2C-ATS) and a curvature-aware variant (LTE-ATS), and benchmark all three against uniform and static priors on CIFAR-10 under a shared U-Net backbone, analyzing their effect on training efficiency (E1), learning dynamics (E2), and inference geometry (E3).

Our comparison suite shows that the advantages of ATS do not transfer to CondOT Flow Matching. Adaptive timestep sampling matches uniform to within run-to-run variation (FID 14.18 versus 13.83) while adding roughly 10% wall-clock training time, because the loss-reduction reward on CondOT paths is minuscule and decays to near zero as the backbone converges. This leaves no stable non-uniform optimum and deprives the A2C critic of the structure it would need to lower variance. We further find the per-timestep loss and gradient-variance profile to be structured but to settle into a stationary U-shape after the first epochs (E2), so that a static prior already captures the exploitable structure effectively. This structure is directional: over-allocating training to the noise-proximal regime proves actively harmful to sample quality (FID up to +3.51 relative to uniform), whereas a data-biased prior does not degrade it. The curvature-driven LTE-ATS collapses entirely (FID 395.2), since the marginal curvature its reward targets is unreachable by the conditional objective, consistent with the E3 finding that training-time biases leave the geometry of the learned flow unchanged.

Beyond this negative transfer result, the U-shaped, largely stationary account of per-timestep difficulty and the finding that noise-biased sampling degrades sample quality advance the understanding of Flow Matching training dynamics and inform the principled design of future timestep-sampling methods.

What the thesis found

  • Adaptive sampling does not transfer. Reinforcement-learned timestep sampling matches uniform sampling to within run-to-run variation (FID 14.18 vs 13.83) and costs about 10% more wall-clock time.
  • The reward signal dies. On CondOT paths the loss-reduction reward is minuscule and decays toward zero as the backbone converges, leaving no stable non-uniform optimum — and starving the A2C critic of the structure it would need for variance reduction.
  • The difficulty profile is real but static. Per-timestep loss and gradient variance settle into a stationary U-shape after the first epochs, which is exactly what a fixed prior can already exploit.
  • Direction matters more than adaptivity. Over-allocating training to the noise-proximal regime is actively harmful (up to +3.51 FID); a data-biased prior does no damage.
  • Curvature-aware rewards collapse. LTE-ATS reaches FID 395.2 — the marginal curvature its reward targets is simply unreachable through the conditional objective.
  • Training-time bias leaves inference geometry untouched, which is why the sampling schedule cannot buy what the objective does not encode.
