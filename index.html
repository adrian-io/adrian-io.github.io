Research

Two lines of work so far: Bayesian statistics and forecasting with honest uncertainty, and generative modelling in deep learning.

My statistical work, from energy price forecasting to Bayesian structure learning and crisis early warning, has been about predictive intervals rather than point estimates. My master's thesis studied how flow-matching models should spend their training compute across the timestep axis. In a PhD I want to make deep learning honest about its uncertainty: separate aleatoric from epistemic uncertainty, keep it calibrated, and make it scale, with energy systems and medicine as applications.

Adaptive timestep sampling for flow matching

Master's thesis at CompVis. Does reinforcement-learned timestep sampling transfer from denoising diffusion to conditional optimal-transport flow matching? Result: no — adaptive sampling matches uniform within run-to-run variation (FID 14.18 vs 13.83) for ~10% more wall-clock, because the loss-reduction reward decays to nothing as the backbone converges. The per-timestep difficulty profile turns out to be U-shaped and stationary, and noise-biased sampling actively hurts sample quality.

Electricity demand forecasting for Spain

Problem
Monthly demand has a trend, two seasonal peaks, moving holidays and breaks such as the 2009 slump.
Method
A SARIMA search over 550 specifications, then ARIMAX with trading-day and Easter effects and outlier correction: the 2019 hold-out MAPE falls from 2.25% to 1.73%.
Stack
R

PISA scores from country indicators

Problem
Predicting a country's PISA results in maths, reading and science from economic and social indicators alone.
Method
Pooled OLS on OECD and World Bank panel data with standard errors clustered by country, and regressors added by cross-validated forward selection: out-of-sample R² of 0.78 for maths, 0.70 for reading and 0.72 for science.
Stack
Python, linearmodels, scikit-learn, pandas
Code

Health insurance cost prediction

Problem
What medical costs a health insurer should expect from each policyholder, given age, BMI, smoking and a few other attributes.
Method
K-means splits 1,338 policyholders into three cost groups; classifiers predict each person's group with 92% accuracy, and regressions that use it edge out the best single model (test RMSE 4,380 vs 4,434).
Stack
Python, scikit-learn, Keras, pandas

About

I came to machine learning through industrial engineering at KIT, and to statistics through a Foreign Office project on predicting armed conflict — which is where I learned that a forecast without an honest interval is a liability rather than an asset.

Since then: an Elite Master's in Data Science at LMU Munich with an exchange semester in Barcelona, a thesis in the Computer Vision & Learning group on how flow-matching models should spend their training budget, and four years of applied work — data warehouses at FINN, retrieval-augmented chatbots at Scalable Capital, crisis early warning in Berlin.

What holds it together is a preference for models that are explicit about their own uncertainty. That is the thread I want to keep pulling in a PhD, preferably where the answer matters, such as energy systems and medicine.
